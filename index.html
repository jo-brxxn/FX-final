❓ How FX Analyst Pro works

The score
Every indicator (CPI, NFP, PMI …) carries a bias: ▲ Bullish +1, ◆ Neutral 0, ▼ Bearish −1, from the release beating or missing its forecast. Indicators with a Core twin (e.g. CPI + Core CPI) count ±0.5 each so the theme isn't double-weighted; the same half weight applies to bond yields, COT net positioning and CB Tone. Most biases update automatically from calendar releases; manual clicks stick only until the next release. If a release has no forecast to compare against, the direction against the previous value sets the bias instead, counted at ±0.5 — half strength, because it is a weaker signal. Marking an indicator ★ important adds +0.5 on top. A release that is more than 2 of its own cycles overdue counts 0 and is marked OUT OF DATE — measured against its own release rhythm, so quarterly reporters are not penalised. The 2Y/10Y spread is display-only and always counts 0.
What is shown but deliberately NOT scored: the 📈 2-step momentum trend and revisions of the previous value. Both were part of the score until August 2026 and were taken out: the trend had grown into an equal-ranked driver rather than the intended bonus (it accounted for 47% of the USD score and 100% of AUD's), and Citi's CESI leaves a trend term out for the same reason — surprise and momentum act on different horizons. Revisions arrived unreliably (the source carries them for about three days only) and their effect depended on reporting frequency rather than on content. Both still appear on the card, with their colour, because they tell you something — they just no longer move the number.
The weighting switch in the header changes how the points are combined. Classic is the plain sum described above, recomputable in your head. Normalised multiplies each indicator by three measured factors: how big the surprise is in units of that indicator's own historical forecast error (this is what makes NFP, σ ≈ 75,700, comparable to CPI, σ ≈ 0.12), how old the release is (half-life 1.5 of its own cycles), and how much the price actually moved on that indicator's release days in the past. The product is clamped to 0.4–1.8 and centred on 1.0 so the ±2/±3 thresholds keep their meaning. Nothing is assigned by hand; "Data quality" in the asset header shows every number behind it.
Card score = sum of its indicators. A card turns bullish at ≥ +2 and bearish at ≤ −2. Symbol score = sum of all cards; the symbol turns bullish at ≥ +3 / bearish at ≤ −3.
Pairs & set-ups
Pair score = base score − quote score + carry (e.g. EUR −5, USD +5 → EUR/USD −10). The Δ%-badge is the policy-rate differential: it adds ±0.5 to the pair score from ±0.5% and ±1 from ±1.5% — positive carry favours being long the pair. Tap/click the badge for a breakdown (both policy rates, the differential and the exact score effect). The Set-ups tab sorts all pairs into Bullish / Neutral / Bearish columns from that.
Central bank & COT
The Interest Rates card has two extra indicators. CB Tone: your own read of the central bank's rhetoric — set it ▲ Bullish for hawkish, ▼ Bearish for dovish (counts at half weight, like a bond yield). Next CB Move: your expected next decision — ▲ Bullish for hike, ▼ Bearish for cut, ◆ Neutral for hold (full weight). Both are manual and stick until you change them. COT net positioning counts ±0.5 once one side holds ≥60% of speculative positions; the week-over-week change counts ±1 only when the shift is at least 3 percentage points away from 0 — smaller moves count ±0.5. The 2Y/10Y Spread is display-only and counts 0 — a 10Y move would otherwise be double-counted, and curve steepening isn't a clean bullish/bearish signal on its own.
